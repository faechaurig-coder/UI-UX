# KITTY PROJECT — PRODUCT BIBLE

## Product statement

A cozy mobile game about finding vulnerable cats, helping them through tactile puzzles, earning their trust, rescuing them and building a living home where their personalities continue to unfold.

## One-line fantasy

> **I rescue cats, earn their trust, bring them home, and discover who they become when they finally feel safe.**

## What the player should feel

Not:
- “I completed level 20.”
- “I unlocked rare cat #07.”
- “I filled a collection slot.”

Instead:
- “Mochi finally came out of the box.”
- “He sleeps in the chair I picked.”
- “He was scared of me before.”
- “He did something funny while I was gone.”
- “That toy is perfect for him.”
- “I want to see him again.”

---

# Emotional loop

**DISCOVER → CARE → HELP → TRUST → RESCUE → BOND → EXPRESS → OBSERVE → MISS → RETURN**

This is the strategic heart of the product.

---

# Core loop

**HOME → RESCUE/STORY GOAL → PUZZLE → VISIBLE PROGRESS → RESCUE/RESOURCE → HOME CHANGE → CAT REACTION → MEMORY/EXPRESSION → NEXT DESIRE**

The player should always understand why the next puzzle matters.

---

# Meta loop

**CAT → RELATIONSHIP → HOME → COLLECTION → NEW CAT → NEW RELATIONSHIPS → NEW BEHAVIORS → DEEPER HOME**

---

# Mochi

Mochi is the first proof of product.

## Required role
- vulnerable but not pathetic;
- cautious but curious;
- visually iconic;
- expressive;
- capable of warmth without instantly loving the player.

## Relationship arc

### 0 — Unknown
Only eyes, sound, movement.

### 1 — Aware
Mochi watches the player.

### 2 — Tolerates help
Accepts food/warmth but keeps distance.

### 3 — Tests safety
Approaches, retreats, observes.

### 4 — Rescue
Allows the player to help fully.

### 5 — New home
Overwhelmed by environment; hides.

### 6 — First comfort
Chooses a spot.

### 7 — First voluntary approach
Important future milestone.

### 8 — Bonded
Not “owned”; safe and familiar.

---

# The Box Moment

The arrival home must be a signature scene.

## Rules
- no reward carousel;
- no 5 currencies;
- no confetti storm;
- no forced tutorial overlays;
- no instant outfit screen.

Use pacing.

The player should have room to watch.

The cat is the reward.

---

# Living-room philosophy

The room is not a menu background.

It is a simulation space.

## It must support
- wandering;
- resting;
- looking outside;
- hiding;
- toy interactions;
- object preferences;
- player attention;
- small surprises;
- later cat-to-cat interactions.

## Important
Quiet behavior is content.

A cat sleeping peacefully for 20 seconds can be more emotionally convincing than a constant stream of exaggerated animations.

---

# Attachment without guilt

Do not turn the game into work.

Avoid:
- urgent hunger;
- thirst decay;
- dirty litter obligations;
- illness from absence;
- punishment for not logging in;
- “Mochi is sad because you left” manipulation.

Prefer:
- positive surprises on return;
- small discoveries;
- new favorite spots;
- photos/memories;
- environmental reactions;
- affection milestones.

---

# Puzzle philosophy

The match-3 system may survive, but only if it earns its place.

## The board should feel like part of the world

Examples:
- food pieces;
- blanket patches;
- cardboard/shelter pieces;
- drops;
- pawprints;
- toys;
- safe-path obstacles.

## Puzzle goals
Progress must visibly change something outside the board.

For example:
- collect blanket pieces → box gains a dry cover;
- clear rain blockers → water recedes;
- collect food → Mochi eats;
- clear debris → safe path opens.

## Avoid
“Collect 30 purple pieces because the mission says purple means food.”

---

# Home collection model

Exactly four MVP territories:

## CATBOOK
Who they are.

Includes:
- rescue story;
- personality;
- bond;
- likes;
- discovered behaviors.

## MEMORIES
What happened.

Includes:
- first meeting;
- box moment;
- first sleep;
- funny interactions;
- photos;
- special events.

## HOME
Where life happens.

Includes:
- furniture;
- rest spots;
- toys;
- scratching;
- interactive decoration.

## STYLE
How the player expresses affection.

Includes:
- collars;
- tags;
- small accessories;
- selected light outfits.

---

# Monetization principles

## Good purchase thought
> “Mochi would look adorable with this.”

> “I want this chair because he loves sleeping there.”

> “This room theme feels like us.”

## Bad purchase thought
> “I have to pay because the game blocked me.”

### Primary monetizable surfaces
- home aesthetics;
- interactive furniture;
- collars;
- accessories;
- premium cosmetic sets;
- seasonal visual content;
- optional memory presentation;
- later pass structures only if value is clear.

### Avoid as primary
- lives;
- energy;
- punitive cooldowns;
- gacha rescue;
- aggressive fail/revive;
- predatory scarcity.

---

# Viral design

Do not bribe sharing.

Create shareable events:
- first rescue;
- box exit;
- first voluntary cuddle;
- bizarre sleeping position;
- two cats sharing an object;
- funny toy failure;
- favorite outfit;
- rare spontaneous room event.

Share language should feel like:
> “Look what Mochi did.”

Not:
> “Share to earn 100 coins.”

---

# Art direction

## Style target
Premium cozy stylization.

### Shapes
- round;
- soft;
- readable;
- minimal sharp corners.

### Materials
- matte;
- warm;
- tactile;
- fabric/cardboard/wood should feel distinct.

### Lighting
- soft directional warmth;
- controlled highlights;
- readable cat silhouette.

### Color
Use a disciplined palette.

The cats must remain readable against the room.

### UI
The UI should support the world, not dominate it.

---

# Character design standard

Each cat needs:
- silhouette;
- palette;
- eye language;
- ear language;
- tail language;
- idle vocabulary;
- motion rhythm;
- emotional archetype.

Characters should differ through behavior, not only fur.

---

# Motion language

## Cats
Naturalistic stylization:
- micro-pauses;
- anticipation;
- asymmetry;
- sudden curiosity;
- slow comfort.

## UI
- spring only when appropriate;
- soft easing;
- very little unnecessary bounce.

## Puzzle
- immediate touch;
- crisp swaps;
- readable cascades;
- satisfying specials;
- controlled screen shake.

---

# Audio language

Target:
**cozy with headphones.**

Important layers:
- cardboard;
- fabric;
- paws;
- soft room ambience;
- rain;
- window;
- purr;
- meow variation;
- toy sounds;
- understated UI;
- match/cascade layers.

Silence is also a tool.

---

# Haptics

Use sparingly:
- select;
- valid match;
- special piece;
- large combo;
- rescue confirmation;
- box moment;
- meaningful affection.

---

# Product constraints

Before attachment validation:
- max 1 room;
- max 1 production-ready cat;
- no mass level production;
- no clans;
- no PvP;
- no giant crafting;
- no seven currencies;
- no 20 feature tabs;
- no LiveOps factory.

---

# Success statement

The vertical slice succeeds when players stop describing the experience as:

> “a cute cat puzzle”

and start saying something closer to:

> “I rescued this little cat called Mochi and now he lives in my room.”

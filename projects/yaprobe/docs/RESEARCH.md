# Competitive research — 2026-09-28

## Market map

### Vivino
Strong proof that scan → ratings → personal history → recommendations can become a large consumer product. Its strengths are product recognition, community density, personal history and decision support. Recent user complaints show risks we should explicitly avoid: slower scanning, reduced price visibility, clutter, trust concerns around recommendations/ratings and premium gating of previously free utility.

### Untappd
Strong proof that logging products can become habitual when the catalog and community are dense. Strengths: check-ins, discovery, identity and collection. Weaknesses repeatedly reported by users: clutter, weak search/navigation, excessive badge noise and gamification that can distract from the core task.

### Beli
Shows the power of turning ratings into a personal ranked list and personalized recommendations. It also demonstrates that asking for too much information can trigger privacy/friction objections.

### Blunt: Rate Everything
Shows that a broad “rate everything” proposition can achieve meaningful distribution when it adds search, sentiment, sources and discovery. It is broader than our intended use case.

### Rate Anything
Shows the opposite: a simple generic custom-category rating notebook has very limited distribution. Lesson: “stars + categories” is not a sufficient product loop.

### Scanivo / Barcodim / similar indie products
Independent builders have independently described the exact recurring pain: forgetting that they already tried/bought an item and later repurchasing it. Barcode scanning plus personal notes solves a real problem, but community-first products face the classic cold-start problem.

## What appears to work
- Immediate recognition of a real-world object.
- A useful answer at the moment of purchase.
- Dense personal history.
- Search that behaves exactly as users expect.
- Community data only when it improves a decision.
- Personalized recommendations after enough personal signals.
- Product-specific structure rather than generic free-form categories.
- A strong personal “collection” or memory that gets more useful over time.

## What repeatedly fails or irritates
- Generic “rate anything” without a recurring trigger.
- Forcing account creation before first value.
- Long forms.
- Feed-first design.
- Too many tabs.
- Bad search.
- Product identification failures.
- Hiding previously free core utility behind a paywall.
- Gamification that produces noise instead of value.
- Ratings that can be manipulated without visible confidence/trust signals.
- Mixing commerce/promoted results with ratings without clear disclosure.

## White space
The opportunity is not another review network. It is:

### **A personal product memory that becomes a consumer intelligence network.**

The wedge:
1. “Have I tried this?”
2. “Did I like it?”
3. “Would I buy it again?”
4. “What did I pay?”
5. “What do people like me think?”
6. “Is this good value?”

## Product rules derived from research
- Scan must be visible on every primary screen.
- Personal answer is shown before community opinion when the user has rated the item.
- Search and barcode are equal-quality paths.
- Never make users write a review.
- Never require a photo if a canonical product already exists.
- Avoid daily streaks; they can create artificial consumption pressure and are not needed for the core memory loop.
- Never label a low-sample community score as authoritative.
- Keep historical price visible to the user.
- Separate promoted/affiliate content from organic ratings if commerce is added later.
